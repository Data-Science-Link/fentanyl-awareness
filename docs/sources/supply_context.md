# Where fentanyl comes from, in DEA's words

This note replaces a map of cartel territory. DEA's public threat assessment names organizations and a border entry point. It does not give a dataset of territory, and a drawn territory map would be a judgment rather than a count. The seizure map on the site uses Customs and Border Protection's own totals by region.

Source: U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration, *2025 National Drug Threat Assessment*, DEA-DCT-DIR-007-25, published May 2025, date of information April 2025. PDF: https://www.dea.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/2025NationalDrugThreatAssessment.pdf

## Who DEA names

From the Letter from the Acting Administrator:

> The Sinaloa Cartel and Jalisco New Generation Cartel, now designated along with six other transnational criminal organizations as foreign terrorist organizations, are the primary groups producing the illicit synthetic drugs driving U.S. drug poisoning deaths and trafficking these drugs into the United States.

## Where DEA says it enters

From the Fentanyl section:

> Mexican TCOs dominate fentanyl transportation into and through the United States, with the Southwest Border (SWB) as the main entry point for fentanyl entering the United States.

The same section, citing the El Paso Intelligence Center National Seizure System (figure labeled NIIPG-021-25, as of March 25, 2025):

> U.S. law enforcement seized a total of 14,069 kilograms of fentanyl at the SWB in 2024, the majority at the Arizona-Mexico border.

That kilogram figure is DEA's citation of the National Seizure System. It is not the same series as the Customs and Border Protection pounds on this site. The two should not be added together.
