About fentanyl

What fentanyl is

Fentanyl is a synthetic opioid. In medicine it is used for severe pain. The deaths in this crisis are tied to illicit fentanyl, which is often pressed into counterfeit pills or mixed into other drugs. A very small amount can be fatal. DEA’s fentanyl fact sheet describes that difference.

The counts here are not fentanyl alone. CDC publishes them as synthetic opioids, excluding methadone. That group includes fentanyl and other synthetics such as tramadol. CDC does not publish a public series of fentanyl use, so this site tracks deaths. CDC’s overdose page explains why recent months are still preliminary.

What reaches the United States

DEA’s 2025 National Drug Threat Assessment names the groups producing these drugs and the main entry point. The assessment does not publish a map file, so no territory map is drawn here.

The Sinaloa Cartel and Jalisco New Generation Cartel … are the primary groups producing the illicit synthetic drugs driving U.S. drug poisoning deaths and trafficking these drugs into the United States.

Mexican TCOs dominate fentanyl transportation into and through the United States, with the Southwest Border (SWB) as the main entry point for fentanyl entering the United States.

Source: DEA National Drug Threat Assessment, with page context.
