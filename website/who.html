These counts are people

The reason to assemble this data is the lives behind it. Families submitted the names below to a public DEA memorial. Age and race come from finished death certificates for the last final year. Later sections show what public agencies report about seizures and budgets. None of that is a full account of who is responsible.

People whose families shared a photo with DEA

These first names, ages, states, and photos were submitted to DEA's Faces of Fentanyl exhibit. Families acknowledge DEA's privacy policy, and they can ask DEA to remove a photo. This site does not copy the image files. If a photo leaves the exhibit, it leaves this page on the next update. The number of names is the number of photos families sent to DEA, not the number of deaths. The public listing does not include a video address, so this page links to the exhibit rather than to a video.

Source: the exhibit listing (names and DEA image links, not image files). Photos stay on DEA's site.

What DEA's threat assessment says

Quoted from the 2025 National Drug Threat Assessment. A territory map is not drawn here, because DEA did not publish one as a dataset. A drawn map of cartel territory, supply routes, or border crossings would be a guess.

The Sinaloa Cartel and Jalisco New Generation Cartel … are the primary groups producing the illicit synthetic drugs driving U.S. drug poisoning deaths and trafficking these drugs into the United States.

Mexican TCOs dominate fentanyl transportation into and through the United States, with the Southwest Border (SWB) as the main entry point for fentanyl entering the United States.

Source: the quotations, with page context. The seizure charts below are Customs and Border Protection's pounds. They are a separate series from the kilogram figure in the DEA report. Do not add them together.
