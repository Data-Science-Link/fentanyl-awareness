What this is

What is fentanyl, and why does it matter?

Fentanyl is a synthetic opioid. Pharmaceutical fentanyl is used in medicine for severe pain. The increase in deaths over the last decade is tied to illicit fentanyl, which is often pressed into pills or mixed into other drugs. DEA's fentanyl fact sheet describes that difference.

The death counts on this site are not a fentanyl-only number. CDC publishes them as synthetic opioids, excluding methadone. That group includes fentanyl and other synthetics such as tramadol. CDC's overdose page explains why recent months are still provisional.

Deaths per 1,000 people per year

The same formula is used for every bar: deaths ÷ years ÷ population × 1,000. War rows are U.S. service-member deaths, including deaths from disease and other causes that were not battle deaths. They are not death certificates. The Vietnam bar is short because those deaths are spread from 1955 to 1975, not across one year of fighting. COVID-19 is death certificates that mention COVID-19. The 2024 bar is the synthetic-opioid category, for one year. These are different kinds of death. The chart only lines up the rate.
