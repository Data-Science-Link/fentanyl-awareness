How many people are dying, and did that change after official actions?

These pages use public death records, seizure totals, and family photos that were submitted to a DEA memorial. The drug category in the death records includes fentanyl and other synthetic opioids. It does not include methadone, and it is not a fentanyl-only count.

Why this matters

Fentanyl is a synthetic opioid. Pharmaceutical fentanyl is used in medicine for severe pain. The increase in deaths over the last decade is tied to illicit fentanyl, which is often pressed into pills or mixed into other drugs. DEA's fentanyl fact sheet describes that difference. CDC publishes the death counts used here as "synthetic opioids, excluding methadone." CDC's overdose page explains why recent months are still provisional.

Are deaths rising or falling?

Each point is the estimated number of people who died that month. Where CDC has finished the death certificates, the point is that official count. After the last finished year, the point is estimated from the change in CDC's 12-month total. The vertical lines are dates of official actions. A line is a date, not proof the action caused a change.

A month after the last finished year can move when CDC revises a provisional total. The estimate uses one kind of CDC total all the way through, so a switch from the reported total to the predicted total is not counted as deaths in a single month. If an estimate falls below zero, the chart leaves a gap. That gap is a revision, not a month with no deaths.

This map is the percent change in the 12-month total, not the number of deaths in that month. ColumnWhat it means
MonthThe calendar month, written as year-month.
Estimated deathsDeaths that month. See the next column for whether it is an official count or an estimate.
How this number was producedEither "Official final count" or "Estimated from the change in the 12-month total."
Deaths per 100,000Deaths that month for every 100,000 people. This is not a yearly rate.
Change from the previous monthThis month's deaths minus the month before.
Change from the same month a year earlierThis month's deaths minus that month one year earlier.
Change in the 12-month totalThe arithmetic CDC's rolling total implies. It equals this month minus the same month a year earlier when both totals are complete. Kept so the estimate can be checked.
Official actions that monthActions whose source date falls in the month. Blank means none in the list.
SourceWhere the death number came from.
NoteWhat the category includes, and a warning if an estimate fell below zero.

A number below zero is not a count of deaths. It means a provisional total was revised. Deaths per 100,000 use a July 1 Census population. When that population is from a different year than the deaths, the note on the row names the Census year. An earlier Census year is carried forward when the new year is not in the file yet. The method is written out in monthly_deaths.md.
