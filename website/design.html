Layout

How this site is laid out

This page is the layout note. The questions, and their order, come from the project brief. The same note is in the repository as public-site-layout.md.

What we used as a guide

Strong public-data sites separate a question, a chart, and a source. They treat a death count as people.

  • Our World in Data puts one question at the top, the chart under it, and the source on the line under the chart. The download sits with the article.
  • Office for National Statistics bulletins open with the question, then the latest figure, then a contents list. A chart has a written equivalent.
  • The COVID Tracking Project and the early New York Times coronavirus pages used a useful order: what is happening, then where, then who. They also showed the failure mode. One page becomes a wall of charts.
  • The Marshall Project, The Trace, and the Washington Post Fatal Force database show names, dates, and a way to look someone up. They do not animate a death.
  • Periscopic’s “U.S. Gun Deaths” is the counterexample. The falling arcs made the deaths a spectacle. This site does not do that.
  • Financial Times and Datawrapper charts use few colors, and a dashed line stays different from a solid line in black and white.
  • CDC data portals are where the numbers come from. They are a poor first page for a new reader.

Five pages, in the order of the story

The site starts with why it exists: the crisis was hard to measure, and the deaths are people. A single page gave every question the same weight. On a phone, the download sat under the memorial and the charts. Each page has its own title and a next link. Agency announcements stay available, linked from the policy page and the download, and are not one of the five story pages.

“Use” in the brief is answered with deaths. CDC does not publish a public series of fentanyl use. The pages say the death category includes fentanyl and is not fentanyl alone.

How a chart is built

Every visual has the same parts: a title, a sentence that says how to read it, the chart, a table of the same numbers, and a source line.

Type is a serif for the question and the prose, and a sans-serif for navigation, numbers, tables, and charts. They are system fonts.

Color is a paper background and ink text. One dark red marks the subject. On the map only, green means the 12-month total fell and dark red means it rose. Gray means CDC did not publish a number. Finished months are a solid line. Preliminary months are a dashed line. Public policy dates are vertical lines. The legend says this in words. The old deaths-per-1,000 comparison with wars is no longer the front page. Those inputs remain in comparison_per_1000.csv for anyone who wants the scale calculation.

Three pictures from the brief are not drawn. DEA’s threat assessment does not publish a territory file, so a cartel map, a supply-chain map, or a crossings map would be a guess. The site quotes DEA and shows Customs and Border Protection’s pounds by region and by component. The share of drug-poisoning deaths is a number and a single bar, not a pie, because one death can list more than one drug.

The public memorial listing has a first name, an age, a state, and a DEA photo address. It does not include a video address. The site links to the exhibit.

Engagement without a spectacle

The engaging part is a question and a sentence that answers it. There is no counting animation and no autoplay. The memorial is a directory. Photos are shown in small groups, uncropped, and a control above them hides the photos without hiding the names. A change after an official action is two counts, not a verdict.

Phone, keyboard, and screen reader

The header collapses to a Menu button. Targets are at least 44 pixels tall. Charts stack in one column. The map is followed by a written list and a full table. Each page starts with a skip link. The current page is marked. Charts are labeled, and the table next to them is the text equivalent. If the reader asks for less motion, the charts do not animate.

Start with why this site exists

Each count is a person. This site exists so the loss stays visible, and so it is easier to see whether anything is changing.

Confirm figures with the agency that published them before using them for a decision.
